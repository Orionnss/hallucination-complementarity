\section{Voter-count ablation}
\label{sec:appendix:voters}

Every subset of the five detectors is evaluated under the three voting rules, giving
$2^5-1 = 31$ combinations per rule, per generator, per dataset. All figures are pooled over
the four QA datasets, averaged over 5 seeds, and computed on stage~3's stored out-of-fold
predictions --- each detector under its own published probe, with nothing refitted.

The $k{=}1$ rows are the detectors' published figures: MCC from the stored decision, which
uses that detector's own published threshold, and AUROC from its continuous score. This is
not what the voting code produces at $k{=}1$, and the difference matters. Soft and rank
voting re-threshold the single detector by the rule the combiners use, which raises
SAPLMA's pooled MCC on Qwen3-14B from $0.4827$ to $0.5089$; hard voting keeps the published
threshold but computes AUROC from a binary vector, giving $0.7497$ instead of $0.8357$.
Taking $k{=}1$ from stage~3 directly avoids both artefacts, so the $k{=}1 \rightarrow k{=}2$
step measures the effect of adding a voter rather than the effect of changing the threshold
rule.

\subsection{Marginal contribution of each voter}

\emph{Remove} is the full five-voter ensemble minus that voter: the cost of
dropping it. \emph{Shapley} is its mean marginal contribution over all 16 coalitions that
exclude it; with five players this is exact rather than sampled, and the five values sum to
the full ensemble's score in every cell. The two disagree when a voter is redundant with
another --- it can be cheap to remove, because a near-duplicate covers for it, and still
contribute where that duplicate is absent. These values are computed inside the sweep, so
their $k{=}1$ term is the re-thresholded one described above; they measure contribution
within the ensemble and are not single-detector performance.
