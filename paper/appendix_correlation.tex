\section{Correlation and voter-ablation details}
\label{sec:correlation_details}

All figures below use each detector under its own published probe, on the
out-of-fold predictions of stage~3's nested cross-validation, averaged over 5 seeds. Four
agreement metrics are reported because they do not measure the same object: Cohen's
$\kappa$ and raw agreement are computed on the binary decisions, Pearson $r$ and
Spearman $\rho$ on the continuous scores. The first pair answers whether two detectors
make the same calls; the second, whether they order items the same way.

Pearson and Spearman never differ by more than $0.02$ in any cell, so the
detectors' scores are monotonically related and none is a rescaling of another. Raw

agree on 82--91\% of decisions while $\kappa$ falls to 0.04--0.15. With a base rate near
13\%, both mostly predict ``not hallucinated'' and match by default. $\kappa$ removes that
and shows near-independence, so reporting agreement alone would invert the conclusion.

\subsection{Voter ablation: cost of removal and marginal contribution}

Two quantities per voter. \emph{Remove} is the full five-voter vote minus that
voter: the cost of dropping it from a deployed ensemble. \emph{Shapley} is its mean
marginal contribution over all 16 coalitions that exclude it; with five players this is
exact rather than sampled, so the five values sum to the full vote's score, which holds in
every cell and validates the enumeration. The two disagree whenever a voter is redundant
with another: a voter can be cheap to remove, because a near-duplicate covers for it, and
still contribute substantially across the subsets where that duplicate is absent.

Only SAPLMA is expensive to remove, at $+0.0295$ MCC on average; every other
voter costs between $-0.0015$ and $+0.0092$. On Gemma3-12B (base) LapEigVals is costlier
than SAPLMA ($+0.0163$ against $+0.0141$), the one model where SAPLMA is not the strongest
single detector. In 9 of the 30 model$\times$voter cells the remove cost is
non-positive: dropping that voter leaves the ensemble no worse. ICR is the clearest case
of the two measures disagreeing --- its mean remove cost is $-0.0015$ while its Shapley
value is $+0.0854$, so it contributes when SAPLMA is absent and contributes nothing when
SAPLMA is present.
