\paragraph{Agreement on Another Detector's Errors:}
\tablename~\ref{tab:conditional_kappa} restricts each detector to the items another
detector got wrong and measures its agreement with the judge label there. This asks the
question combination depends on: when the strongest detector fails, does any other one
still know the answer? The observed values are almost all negative, but they cannot be
read against zero. Conditioning on one detector's errors is collider conditioning, so any
detector correlated with the first looks poor on that slice regardless of its own quality,
and the slice's class balance shifts, which moves every metric by itself. We therefore
pair each cell with a null obtained by permuting the evaluated detector within each class,
preserving its per-class accuracy and score distribution exactly while destroying any
dependence on the first detector. An equally strong but independent detector scores
$\kappa \in [0.16, 0.55]$ on these slices, not $0$. Against that reference the observed
values are far worse than they appear: averaged over the 20 ordered pairs in each model,
the gap is $-0.504$ in $\kappa$ and $-0.592$ in Pearson $r$
(\tablename~\ref{tab:conditional_gap}), and \emph{no} pair in any model on any of the four
metrics reaches its null --- the least damaged cell across all 120 pairs still falls
$0.175$ short in $\kappa$. The four metrics agree, which was not guaranteed: $\kappa$ and
agreement use the thresholded decisions while Pearson and Spearman use the continuous
scores, so a detector could in principle rank the error slice well and merely threshold it
badly. It does not. Pearson and Spearman are within $0.023$ of each other throughout, so
the relationship is monotone and no non-linear score mapping conceals the redundancy. The
ordering of rows is the substantive result: SVD, the weakest detector, has the most
recoverable errors in five of six models, while SAPLMA or LapEigVals --- the two
strongest --- have the least recoverable in all six. Failures concentrate where the
detectors are strongest, which is the accuracy--independence tradeoff measured directly
rather than inferred from aggregate performance.
